in [[Physics|physics]], a mechanical wave is a wave that is an oscillation of matter, and therefore transfers energy through a material medium except for when in a vacuum, which is a non-material medium, where electromagnetic waves propagate.

while waves can move over long distances, the movement of the medium of transmission is limited. therefore, the oscillating material does not move far from its initial equilibrium position. mechanical waves can be produced only in media which possess elasticity and inertia. 

there are three types of mechanical waves: [[Transverse waves|transverse waves]], [[Longitudinal waves|longitudinal waves]], and [[Surface waves|surface waves]]. 

some of the most common examples of mechanical waves are water waves, sound waves, and seismic waves.

like all waves, mechanical waves transport energy, this energy propagates in the same direction as the wave. a wave requires an initial energy input. once this initial energy is added, the wave travels through the medium until all its energy is transferred. in contrast, electromagnetic waves require no medium, but can still travel through one.
