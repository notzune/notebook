also known as the Snell-Descartes law, and the law of [[Refraction|refraction]], is a [[Formula|formula]] used to describe the the relationship between the [[Angle of incidence (optics)|angle of incidence]] and refraction when referring to light or other waves passing through a boundary between two different [[Isotropy|isotropic]] media, such as water, glass, or air.

the law states that for a given pair of media, the [[Ratio|ratio]] of the [[Sine and cosine|sines]] of angle of incidence ($\theta_1$) and angle of refraction (\theta_2) is equal to the refractive index of the second medium with regard to the first ($n_2,1$) which is equal to the ratio of the [[Refractive index|refractive indices]] $\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)$ of the two media, or equivalently to the ratio of the [[Phase velocity|phase velocities]] $\left(\frac{v_1}{v_2}\right)$ in the two media.

$$
\frac{\sin\theta_1}{\sin\theta_2}=n2,1=\frac{n_2}{n_1}=\frac{v_1}{v_2}
$$
