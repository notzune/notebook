the angle of incidence, in geometric optics, is the [[Angle|angle]] between a [[Ray|ray incident]] on a surface and the line [[Perpendicularity|perpendicular]] (at ${90}^\circ$ angle) to the surface at the point of incidence, called the [[Normal (geometry)|normal]]. the ray can be formed by any [[Wave|waves]], such as [[Electromagnetic radiation|optical]], [[Sound wave|acoustic]], microwave, and x-ray.

it is sometimes more useful to refer to the angle between the beam and the [[Tangent|surface tangent]], rather than that between the beam and the normal.

the 90-degree [[Complement|complement]] to the angle of incidence is called the grazing angle or glancing angle.

for example, a ray with $30^\circ$ angle of incidence has a $60^\circ$ grazing angle.
